Stitch Cheat Sheet

Keep this page nearby while knitting!

Cast On (Long Tail)

Why: Starts your project with a loop on the needle

Steps:

  1. Make loop about 1.5 feet from yarn end
  2. Slip needle through loop
  3. Pull yarn to tighten
  4. Repeat until you have desired number of stitches

Knit Stitch (K)

How to do it:

  1. Insert right needle front-to-back through first stitch on left needle
  2. Wrap yarn around right needle (counterclockwise)
  3. Pull yarn through to create new loop
  4. Drop old loop off left needle

Tip: Your wrapping speed should match your needle speed. Speed comes with practice.

Purl Stitch (P)

It's the opposite of knit:

  1. Insert right needle back-to-front (opposite of knit)
  2. Wrap yarn around right needle (same way)
  3. Pull yarn through
  4. Drop old loop

Note: Beginners rarely use purl for these patterns, but good to know!

Bind Off

Finishes your project so stitches don't unravel:

  1. Knit 2 stitches
  2. Pass first stitch over second
  3. Knit 1 more
  4. Repeat until 1 stitch remains
  5. Cut yarn and pull through final stitch

Pattern 1: Simple Dishcloth

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 1-2 hours
Finished Size: 8" x 8"

Materials

  • US 8-10 needles (5-6mm)
  • Worsted weight yarn, 200-300 yards

Pattern Instructions

Cast on: 20 stitches

Every row: Knit every stitch (garter stitch)

Continue until: You have roughly 20 rows (piece is square-ish)

Bind off and weave in ends

Notes

  • Your cast-on row will look messy. This is normal and hides in the finished piece.
  • Count rows by looking at bumps on the side of your knitting.
  • Once bound off, weave loose yarn ends through a few stitches to hide them.

Pattern 2: Garter Stitch Scarf

Difficulty: Beginner
Time: 3-5 hours
Finished Size: 6" wide × 60" long

Materials

  • US 8-10 needles
  • Worsted weight yarn, 600-800 yards

Pattern Instructions

Cast on: 18 stitches

Every row: Knit every stitch (garter stitch)

Continue until: You've almost run out of yarn (leave about 3 feet for binding off)

Bind off and weave in ends

What's Different from Dishcloth?

  • More stitches: 18 instead of 20 (makes it narrower/longer)
  • Much longer: You'll knit 200+ rows! This builds stamina and consistency.
  • Same stitch: Still just garter stitch. Same technique, more practice.

Tips for a Better Scarf

  • Keep tension consistent. Aim for medium-tight but not squeezing.
  • Take breaks. Your hands will get tired. This is normal.
  • Your first 10 rows will look sloppy. Keep going. By row 50 it evens out.
  • Try it on periodically to see it taking shape!

Pattern 3: Beanie Hat

Difficulty: Beginner+
Time: 4-6 hours
Finished Size: Adult (fits most heads)

Materials

  • US 8-10 straight needles (for flat knitting)
  • Worsted weight yarn, 400-500 yards
  • Yarn needle (for sewing seam)
  • Optional: 2 buttons or pom-pom

What's New in This Pattern?

  • Seaming: You'll knit two flat panels and sew them together
  • Shaping: You'll learn decreases to make the hat tapered
  • More interesting: It's wearable and gift-worthy!

Pattern Instructions

Main Piece

Cast on: 36 stitches

Rows 1-30: Knit every stitch (garter stitch)

Bind off but don't cut yarn yet. You'll need it for seaming.

Making it a Hat Shape

Step 1: Fold your flat panel in half (so it looks like a rectangle)

Step 2: Sew the two long edges together with yarn needle

Step 3: Sew the top closed, leaving a small opening

Step 4: If desired, add a pom-pom or button on top

Seaming Tutorial

How to sew two pieces together:

  1. Thread yarn needle with yarn
  2. Bring needle through first stitch of one piece
  3. Bring needle through corresponding stitch on other piece
  4. Continue back and forth, creating a seam
  5. Make stitches small and tight so seam doesn't show

Tips

  • Your first seam will look imperfect. That's okay! It gets hidden inside the hat anyway.
  • Try it on as you go to make sure sizing works.
  • If it's too loose, you can tighten it by sewing the seam closer to the edge.

Troubleshooting & Tips

Tension Issues

Problem: Stitches look super tight/loose

Solution: Tension is a learnable skill. By your 3rd pattern, you'll have much better control. For now, just aim for "medium."

Dropped Stitches

Problem: A stitch fell off needle

Solution: Stop immediately. Use a smaller needle or crochet hook to pick it back up. Look for the vertical line of yarn and pull it through.

Uneven Rows

Problem: Some rows look bigger than others

Solution: This happens when your tension changes. Keep practicing. It improves naturally.

Ran Out of Yarn

Problem: Yarn ended before pattern was done

Solution: That's okay! Make your scarf shorter or hat smaller. You can always add more yarn if needed (ask in community).
