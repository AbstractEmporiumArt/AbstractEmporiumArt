Welcome to Knitting

Congratulations on deciding to learn knitting! You're about to start a rewarding hobby that millions of people around the world love.

Why You'll Love Knitting

  • Meditative: Knitting is calming. Your hands stay busy while your mind relaxes.
  • Creative: You make beautiful things. Scarves, hats, blankets—all from your own hands.
  • Affordable: Start with just $5-15 in supplies. Everything else is free.
  • Flexible: Do it anywhere. While watching TV, on the bus, in the park.
  • Social: Join communities of knitters. Share projects. Get support.

Good news: This guide assumes you've never knitted before. Every step is explained clearly. You'll be making your first project in 1-2 hours.

Supplies You'll Need

Here's exactly what to buy for your first project:

1. Needles

Size: US 8-10 (5-6mm needles)

Type: Straight needles (the basic kind)

Material: Bamboo or metal (bamboo won't let yarn slip)

Cost: $2-4 at any craft store

Where: Michaels, Joann, Walmart, Amazon, or local craft store

2. Yarn

Weight: Worsted weight (also called "medium" or "4-ply")

Fiber: 100% acrylic (cheap, easy to knit, machine washable)

Color: Light color (easier to see your stitches)

Amount: You need 200-300 yards

Cost: $3-5

Example: Red Heart "Super Saver" - commonly available everywhere

3. Stitch Markers (Optional but Helpful)

Cost: $2-3 for a set of 10

Alternative: Use safety pins or even yarn in a different color

Total Cost: $7-12

That's it! You can get all of this at a single craft store in 15 minutes.

Pro Tip: Buy acrylic yarn for your first project. It's forgiving, easy to knit, and cheap if you make mistakes.

Basic Techniques

1. How to Hold Your Needles

There are two main ways:

  • Pencil grip: Hold needles like you're writing (most common for beginners)
  • Knife grip: Hold top needle like a knife. This is faster but takes practice.

For now, use pencil grip. It's easier to control.

2. Casting On

What it is: How you put the initial loops of yarn onto your needle.

For this pattern: We'll use the "Long Tail Cast On" (most popular for beginners)

Steps:

  1. Make a loop with your yarn about 1.5 feet from the end
  2. Slip needle through the loop
  3. Pull yarn to tighten
  4. Repeat until you have 25 stitches
Don't worry if your cast-on looks messy. It gets better with practice. What matters is that you have loops on the needle.

3. The Knit Stitch

This is the most basic stitch. Once you master this, you can make almost anything.

Steps:

  1. Hold needle with loops in left hand
  2. Insert right needle through first loop (front to back)
  3. Wrap yarn around right needle
  4. Pull yarn through to create new loop
  5. Drop old loop off left needle
  6. Repeat for all stitches

First row will feel awkward. This is normal. By row 10, muscle memory kicks in and it becomes automatic.

4. Binding Off

What it is: How you finish your project so stitches don't unravel.

Steps:

  1. Knit two stitches
  2. Slip first stitch over second stitch
  3. Knit one more stitch
  4. Repeat until one stitch remains
  5. Cut yarn and pull through final stitch

Your First Pattern: Simple Dishcloth

Pattern Difficulty: Beginner
Time to Complete: 1-2 hours
Finished Size: About 8" x 8"

Materials

  • US 8-10 needles (5-6mm)
  • Worsted weight yarn, light color, 200-300 yards

Pattern

Cast on: 20 stitches using long tail cast on

Row 1: Knit every stitch

Row 2: Knit every stitch

Row 3: Knit every stitch

Row 4: Knit every stitch

Repeat Rows 1-4 until your piece is square (or until you run low on yarn)

Bind off all stitches (see instructions above)

Weave in ends - trim loose yarn threads

What You're Creating

You're making a garter stitch dishcloth. Garter stitch is:

  • The easiest stitch to knit
  • Thick and absorbent (perfect for washing dishes)
  • Looks the same on both sides

Counting Rows

To know when your square is square, count your rows. It should roughly equal the number of stitches across (20 rows for 20 stitches).

Troubleshooting Common Mistakes

Problem: Yarn keeps falling off my needle

Cause: Stitches aren't tight enough

Solution: Pull yarn tighter after each stitch. It'll feel too tight at first, but loosen as you gain control.

Problem: My knitting looks uneven

Cause: Tension is inconsistent. This is 100% normal for beginners.

Solution: Keep practicing. After 50 stitches, you'll notice it evens out. After 100, it'll look much better.

Problem: I dropped a stitch

Solution for small gaps: Don't panic. Use a smaller needle (or crochet hook) to pick up the dropped stitch and pull it back through the loop.

Solution if too far back: Unravel back to that point and reknit. Sounds scary, but it's easier than it sounds.

Problem: My first row looks sloppy

This is completely normal. Cast-on rows are always messy-looking. By row 5, you won't even notice it.

Problem: I think I need to restart

Don't quit! Mistakes are learning. Keep going. It gets easier, I promise.
