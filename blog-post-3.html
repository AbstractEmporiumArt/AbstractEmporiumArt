From "I Can't Draw" to a Gallery Wall — Three Years After the Injury

By Lissa Beaulieu Oct 2026 Freehand / Art Therapy / Gallery

Three years ago, after my workplace injury, I picked up a pen and a sketch pad and decided to try something that felt impossible: freehand drawing. Not tracing. Not copying an outline. Just me, a page, and the terrifying blank white space.

I grew up convinced I couldn't draw. Books, little bookmarks, a messy crayon moment as a kid — that was the extent of my artistic identity. Then the injury changed everything. I lost my work in healthcare. I needed therapy — something to do with my hands that wasn't just survival. That's when the drawing started.

The Messy Practice Phase

At first, everything was practice. Erasing. Starting again. Trying a variety of types — cartoon characters, nature scenes, shapes, something weird I found on my screen (hello, Trailer Park Boys lounging group with a kitten — yes, that drawing is real, and yes, it's in the gallery now).

I kept a sketchbook. I drew through frustration. I drew through pain. I drew when I couldn't sleep. Some days the lines felt wrong and I threw the page away. Other days — maybe one in ten — something clicked.

From Sketchbook to Canvas

Recently I started painting my freehand work onto canvas and sealing it with gloss. That's when things shifted. A sketch isn't just a sketch anymore — it's a finished piece, hanging on a wall, never exactly duplicated.

Some of those pieces are now part of the new Gallery section on Art Emporium: the four original fluid pours, the wood panel set, the freehand drawings (including the Simpsons aliens and the Trailer Park Boys lounging group), and the sold commissions.

The Fun Part

The best part? There's no pressure. I practice by drawing whatever catches my eye — a cartoon, a creature, a scene from a movie. The drawing of the green mecha vehicles? That's actually aliens from The Simpsons, drawn as a freehand study. The lounging group with the kitten? Trailer Park Boys. The South Park cast? Exactly what it looks like.

If you want something cozy to pair with your art exploration, grab a hand-poured candle from Z3NW1CK or browse the knits at Lissa's Knitting Creations. Creative sessions are always better with good light, a warm scent, and something soft to sit on.

A Few Favorites in the Gallery

  • Trailer Park Boys — lounging group, one with a kitten (freehand, painted on canvas, glossed)
  • Aliens from The Simpsons (freehand green mecha study — not mecha, just aliens)
  • The four origin fluid pours (where it all started — raw canvas, no planning)
  • The commissioned painting for my client's music studio (48×60", sold to a dear friend in Alberta)

The gallery isn't finished. It never will be. Every new sketch is a possibility. Every mistake is practice. And every finished piece — even the weird ones — is proof that starting again works.

— Lissa Beaulieu
