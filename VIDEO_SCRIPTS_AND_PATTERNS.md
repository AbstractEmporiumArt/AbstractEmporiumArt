**[00:20 - 00:50] SETUP**
```
"Starting position:
- Needle with stitches in your LEFT hand

- Working yarn coming from the back

Let's break it down slowly..."

[Position hands on camera]
```

**[00:50 - 02:40] DEMONSTRATION**
```
"The Four Steps:

Step 1: INSERT
[Extreme close-up]
Insert your right needle into the first stitch on the left needle.
Go IN from the LEFT side, pointing TOWARD THE RIGHT.
Your needles make an X shape - see that?

Step 2: WRAP
Keep your right needle there.
Take your working yarn (the one attached to the ball).
Wrap it AROUND the right needle, going BETWEEN your two needles.
Counterclockwise - toward you, then away.

Step 3: PULL THROUGH
This is the tricky part - watch carefully!
[Very slow motion]
Use your right needle tip to pull that wrapped yarn THROUGH the old stitch.
You're making a new stitch on your right needle!

Step 4: SLIP OFF
Now slip the old stitch off your left needle.

BOOM! You just made your first knit stitch!

[Show completed stitch]

Let's do it again - INSERT, WRAP, PULL THROUGH, SLIP OFF.

[Demonstrate 5 stitches at normal speed]

See how a row builds? Each stitch you make jumps from left needle to right needle.

When your left needle is empty, you've finished one row!
Turn your work around and start again.

[Show turning work]

Common mistakes:
❌ Wrapping yarn the wrong direction - stitches get twisted
❌ Not pulling yarn tight enough - loose stitches
❌ Pulling TOO tight - can't get needle in next time

[Show each mistake]

The fix? Just practice! Your tension will even out."

[Show 10 stitches knitted smoothly]
```

**[02:40 - 03:00] OUTRO**

### VIDEO 3: The Purl Stitch (3 minutes)
**Filename:** `tutorial-03-purl-stitch.mp4`

**SCRIPT:**

**[00:00 - 00:20] INTRO**
```
"You've mastered the knit stitch - awesome!
Now let's learn its opposite: the PURL stitch.

Knit + Purl together unlock EVERY knitting pattern in existence.

Ready? Let's purl!"

[Show needles with knitting in progress]
```

**[00:20 - 00:50] CONCEPT**
```
"What's the difference?

KNIT: You work from the back of the fabric
PURL: You work from the front

Imagine knit as 'pushing forward' and purl as 'pulling back'.

They're literally the same stitch, just approached from different sides.

Mind. Blown. Right?

[Show side-by-side comparison]
```

**[00:50 - 02:40] DEMONSTRATION**
```
"The Four Steps (similar to knit, but OPPOSITE):

Step 1: INSERT
[Close-up]
Start with yarn IN FRONT (not in back like knit).
Insert right needle into first stitch from RIGHT TO LEFT.
Needle goes in from the front, pointing toward the back.

Step 2: WRAP
With yarn still in front, wrap it AROUND the right needle.
Counterclockwise - same as knit, but from the front position.

Step 3: PULL THROUGH
[Very slow]
Use right needle to pull the wrapped yarn BACK THROUGH the old stitch.
This is backward from knit - you're pulling toward yourself.

Step 4: SLIP OFF
Slip the old stitch off the left needle.

That's one purl stitch!

[Demonstrate 3 times slow, then 5 times at normal speed]

When to use purl?
- Ribbing (K2, P2 pattern makes stretchy cuffs)
- Texture patterns (combining knit + purl creates designs)
- Stockinette stitch (knit one row, purl one row = smooth fabric)

[Show examples of each]

Most patterns alternate: Row 1 knit, Row 2 purl, Row 3 knit...

Pro tip: If your yarn is in front, you're about to purl.
          If your yarn is in back, you're about to knit.

EASY!"

[Show smooth purling for 10 stitches]
```

**[02:40 - 03:00] OUTRO**
```
"You now know KNIT and PURL - the building blocks of everything!

Practice switching between them. Start a row with knit, 
then try purl on the next row.

Next video: How to BIND OFF (finish your project!)"

See all tutorials at abstractemporiumart.com
```

### VIDEO 4: How to Bind Off (Finish Your Project) (3 minutes)
**Filename:** `tutorial-04-bind-off.mp4`

**SCRIPT:**

**[00:00 - 00:20] INTRO**
```
"You've been knitting for a while and your scarf is the perfect length.
Now what?

Time to BIND OFF - the technique that locks your stitches so they don't unravel.

This is the satisfying moment where you finally finish your project!"

[Show almost-complete project]
```

**[00:20 - 00:45] SETUP**
```
"You'll need:
- Your knitting (any number of stitches)
- Working yarn attached
- Scissors for the final cut

Binding off is basically 'knitting your stitches into each other' 
to create a finished edge.

[Show materials]
```

**[00:45 - 02:40] DEMONSTRATION**
```
"The Process:

Step 1: Knit 2 Stitches
Start by knitting the first 2 stitches normally.
[Demonstrate]
Now you have 2 stitches on your right needle.

Step 2: Lift and Pass
[Close-up]
Use your LEFT needle to lift the FIRST stitch you knitted.
Pass it UP AND OVER the second stitch.
Let it fall off the needle.

You just 'bound off' one stitch!

Step 3: Knit 1 More
Now knit the next stitch from your left needle.
You have 2 stitches on your right needle again.

Step 4: Repeat
Lift the first stitch, pass it over the second, drop it off.

[Demonstrate continuously for 10 stitches]

See the pattern?
- Knit one
- Pass the previous stitch over
- Knit one
- Pass over
- Repeat until you have only 1 stitch left

The Final Stitch:
[Show last stitch on needle]

Cut your working yarn, leaving a 6-inch tail.
Pull the tail through the last stitch.
Pull tight - DONE!

[Show excited reaction]

Optional: Weave in that tail using a yarn needle so it doesn't show.

Tips:
- Don't bind off too tight - your edge should stretch like the rest
- Keep consistent tension - all bound-off stitches should be similar size
- If it's too tight, loosen up! If it's too loose, pull a bit more.

[Show good vs. too-tight bind-off]
```

### VIDEO 5: How to Fix Dropped Stitches (4 minutes)
**Filename:** `tutorial-05-fix-dropped-stitches.mp4`

**SCRIPT:**

```

[Show intentionally dropped stitch]
```

**[00:25 - 01:00] IDENTIFYING THE PROBLEM**
```
"What does a dropped stitch look like?

[Show close-up]
See this 'ladder' running down your knitting?
That's your stitch trying to escape!

It happens when you accidentally slip a stitch off your needle 
without knitting it.

The stitch starts to unravel, row by row, creating a vertical gap.

[Point to ladder]

How far did it drop?
- One row down? Super easy fix.
- Three rows down? Still fixable!
- Ten rows down? ...Okay, that's annoying, but still doable.

Let's fix it!"

[Show different ladder lengths]
```

**[01:00 - 03:30] DEMONSTRATION**
```
"You'll need:
- A crochet hook (any size close to your needle size)
- Or a spare knitting needle
- Patience!

THE FIX:

Step 1: SECURE THE STITCH
[Close-up]
Find the 'live' stitch at the bottom of the ladder.
It's the one trying to escape!

Slide your crochet hook through it.
Now it can't unravel further. Safe!

Step 2: PICK UP THE LADDER
[Very slow motion]
See those horizontal bars in the ladder? 
Each one represents a row.

Hook the LOWEST  bar (closest to your live stitch).
Pull it THROUGH the stitch.

That's one row recovered!

Now hook the next bar up and pull it through.

[Demonstrate 3 rows]

Keep going:
- Hook the bar
- Pull through
- Hook the next bar
- Pull through

Until you've worked your way back to the top!

[Show completed stitch]

Step 3: PUT IT BACK ON YOUR NEEDLE
Slide your left needle into the recovered stitch.
Make sure it's facing the right direction - the right 'leg' in front.

DONE! Continue knitting normally.

[Show knitting a few stitches past the fix]

PURL STITCHES:
If you dropped a purl stitch, same technique but work from the BACK.

[Quick demo]

PREVENTION TIPS:
- Use stitch markers to catch mistakes early
- Count your stitches at the end of each row
- Don't knit while distracted (I know, hard!)
- If a stitch looks weird, fix it NOW before it drops

[Show preventive techniques]

The Worst Case:
Dropped it 20 rows down and can't find it?

Option 1: Keep going and fix it later (it'll show a bit)
Option 2: Rip out back to that row and reknit (painful but clean)
Option 3: Call it 'artistic texture' and keep going (my favorite!)

[Laugh]
```

### PATTERN 1: Simple Garter Stitch Dishcloth
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Absolute Beginner  
**Time:** 1-2 hours  
**Finished Size:** 8" × 8"

**MATERIALS:**
- 1 skein (50g) worsted weight cotton yarn
- US Size 7 (4.5mm) needles
- Yarn needle for weaving in ends

**GAUGE:**
Not critical for this project

**ABBREVIATIONS:**
- K = Knit
- CO = Cast On
- BO = Bind Off

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 40 stitches.

Rows 1-60: Knit every stitch.

Bind off all stitches.

Weave in ends.

**FINISHING:**
Block lightly by soaking in warm water, squeeze gently (don't wring), 
lay flat on towel to dry. Cotton will soften with use and washing.

**NOTES:**
This is pure garter stitch - every row you just knit! 
Perfect first project. The bumpy texture is great for scrubbing dishes.

**YARN SUGGESTIONS:**
- Lily Sugar'n Cream (4oz for about $3)
- Bernat Handicrafter Cotton
- Any 100% cotton worsted weight

### PATTERN 2: Beginner's Cozy Scarf
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner  
**Time:** 6-8 hours  
**Finished Size:** 6" × 60"

**MATERIALS:**
- 2 skeins (200g total) worsted weight yarn
- US Size 9 (5.5mm) needles
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
16 stitches = 4 inches in garter stitch

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 24 stitches.

Rows 1-240: Knit every stitch.

Bind off loosely.

**OPTIONAL FRINGE:**
Cut 48 pieces of yarn, each 8 inches long.
At each end of scarf, fold 2 strands in half, pull loop through edge, 
pull ends through loop. Repeat every 2 stitches across width.
Trim fringe evenly.

**VARIATIONS:**
- Stripe it! Change colors every 20 rows
- Make it wider: Cast on 32 stitches instead
- Make it longer: Knit until you run out of yarn!

**NOTES:**
This takes a while, but it's so relaxing. Perfect TV knitting!
Garter stitch never curls, so no blocking needed.

**YARN SUGGESTIONS:**
- Lion Brand Wool-Ease
- Red Heart Super Saver
- Any soft, warm worsted weight

### PATTERN 3: Ribbed Headband
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Easy  
**Time:** 2-3 hours  
**Finished Size:** 4" × 18" (unstretched)

**MATERIALS:**
- 1 skein (100g) worsted weight yarn
- US Size 8 (5mm) needles
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
18 stitches = 4 inches in K2, P2 rib

**ABBREVIATIONS:**
- K = Knit
- P = Purl
- K2 = Knit 2 stitches
- P2 = Purl 2 stitches

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 20 stitches.

Row 1: *K2, P2; repeat from * to end of row.
Row 2: *K2, P2; repeat from * to end of row.

Repeat Rows 1-2 until piece measures 18 inches (or desired length).

Bind off in pattern (knit the knits, purl the purls).

**FINISHING:**
Fold piece in half lengthwise, matching cast-on edge to bind-off edge.
Seam together using whipstitch or mattress stitch.

**NOTES:**
K2, P2 rib creates a stretchy, textured fabric perfect for headbands.
Measure around your head - mine is 22", so 18" unstretched fits perfectly 
when it stretches.

**VARIATIONS:**
- Add a seed bead or button at the seam for decoration
- Make it wider (cast on 28 stitches)
- Use two colors for stripes

### PATTERN 4: Textured Coasters (Set of 4)
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Easy  
**Time:** 30 minutes each  
**Finished Size:** 4" × 4"

**MATERIALS:**
- 1 skein cotton yarn (makes 4-6 coasters)
- US Size 6 (4mm) needles
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
20 stitches = 4 inches

**PATTERN (Seed Stitch):**

Cast on 20 stitches.

Row 1: *K1, P1; repeat from * to end.
Row 2: *P1, K1; repeat from * to end.

Repeat Rows 1-2 until piece is square (about 20 rows).

Bind off.

**FINISHING:**
Weave in ends. Block lightly.

**MAKE IT A SET:**
- Coaster 1: Seed stitch (pattern above)
- Coaster 2: Garter stitch (knit every row)
- Coaster 3: Stockinette (Row 1 knit, Row 2 purl)
- Coaster 4: Ribbing (K2, P2 every row)

**NOTES:**
Cotton is essential - it absorbs moisture and washes well.
These make GREAT gifts!

### PATTERN 5: Baby Blanket for Beginners
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner  
**Time:** 15-20 hours  
**Finished Size:** 30" × 30"

**MATERIALS:**
- 5 skeins (500g total) worsted weight yarn
- US Size 9 (5.5mm) needles (or 24" circular needle)
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
16 stitches = 4 inches

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 120 stitches.

Rows 1-240: Knit every stitch.

Bind off.

**FINISHING:**
Weave in ends.
Block to square shape by pinning edges and lightly steaming.

**BORDER (Optional):**
Pick up stitches around all edges and knit 5 rows of garter stitch 
border. Bind off.

**NOTES:**
This is a BIG project, but the stitch is SIMPLE - just knitting!
Break it into chunks: 20 rows per day = done in 12 days.

Use a circular needle to hold all the stitches comfortably 
(you still knit back and forth, just don't join in a circle).

**YARN SUGGESTIONS:**
- Bernat Baby Blanket (super soft and machine washable)
- Lion Brand Baby Soft
- Any baby-safe, hypoallergenic yarn

### PATTERN 6: Easy Slippers
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Easy  
**Time:** 4-5 hours  
**Finished Size:** Adult medium (adjustable)

**MATERIALS:**
- 2 skeins (200g) bulky weight yarn
- US Size 10 (6mm) needles
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
14 stitches = 4 inches

**PATTERN (make 2):**

SOLE:
Cast on 8 stitches.
Row 1: Knit
Row 2: K1, increase 1, knit to last stitch, increase 1, K1 (10 sts)
Rows 3-4: Knit
Row 5: Increase row (12 sts)
Rows 6-20: Knit
Row 21: Decrease row (10 sts)
Rows 22-23: Knit
Row 24: Decrease row (8 sts)
Bind off.

TOP:
Cast on 30 stitches.
Rows 1-15: Knit
Bind off.

**FINISHING:**
Fold top piece in half and seam short edges together.
Center seam of top onto center of sole.
Sew top to sole around edges, leaving heel end open.

**SIZING:**
- Smaller: Cast on 6 sts for sole, 24 sts for top
- Larger: Cast on 10 sts for sole, 36 sts for top

**NOTES:**
Add non-slip grips to soles using fabric paint dots or 
puffy paint on the bottom!

### PATTERN 7: Phone Case Cozy
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner  
**Time:** 1-2 hours  
**Finished Size:** 3" × 6"

**MATERIALS:**
- Small amount (30g) worsted weight yarn
- US Size 7 (4.5mm) needles
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
18 stitches = 4 inches

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 16 stitches.

Rows 1-40: Knit every row.

Bind off, leaving a long tail for seaming.

**FINISHING:**
Fold piece in half, wrong sides together, matching cast-on to bind-off.
Using long tail, seam up both sides using whipstitch.
Leave top open.
Weave in ends.

**OPTIONAL:**
Add a button loop: Before binding off, work a 5-stitch I-cord for 
3 inches, then bind off. Attach to back, add button to front.

**NOTES:**
Measure your phone first! Adjust stitch count if needed.
This also works great as a cozy for sunglasses, headphones, etc.

### PATTERN 8: Infinity Scarf (Cowl)
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Easy  
**Time:** 8-10 hours  
**Finished Size:** 60" circumference × 8" wide

**MATERIALS:**
- 2-3 skeins (250g) worsted weight yarn
- US Size 10 (6mm) 24" circular needle
- Stitch marker

**GAUGE:**
14 stitches = 4 inches

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 210 stitches.
Place marker and join in the round (being careful not to twist).

Round 1: *K2, P2; repeat from * to end.

Repeat Round 1 until piece measures 8 inches.

Bind off loosely in pattern.

**NOTES:**
This is your first "in the round" project!
The circular needle makes it easy.

When you join, make SURE stitches aren't twisted on the needle.
Place marker to know where rounds begin/end.

You never turn your work - just keep knitting in spiral!

**VARIATIONS:**
- Make it wider: Work for 10-12 inches
- Make it chunky: Use 2 strands held together
- Change stitch pattern: All knit for stockinette, or seed stitch

### PATTERN 9: Knit Mug Cozy
**Difficulty:** ⭐ Beginner  
**Time:** 1 hour  
**Finished Size:** Fits standard mug

**MATERIALS:**
- Small amount (20g) worsted weight cotton yarn
- US Size 7 (4.5mm) needles
- Yarn needle

**GAUGE:**
Not critical

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 40 stitches.

Rows 1-8: *K2, P2; repeat from * to end.

Bind off.

**FINISHING:**

### PATTERN 10: Textured Throw Pillow Cover
**Difficulty:** ⭐⭐ Easy  
**Time:** 12-15 hours  
**Finished Size:** 16" × 16"

**MATERIALS:**
- 3 skeins (300g) worsted weight yarn
- US Size 8 (5mm) needles
- Yarn needle
- 16" pillow form
- 4 buttons (1" diameter)

**GAUGE:**
18 stitches = 4 inches

**PATTERN:**

Cast on 72 stitches.

Rows 1-4: Knit (garter stitch border)
Row 5: K4, *K2, P2; repeat from * to last 4 stitches, K4
Row 6: K4, *P2, K2; repeat from * to last 4 stitches, K4

Repeat Rows 5-6 until piece measures 30 inches from cast on 
(forms front and back when folded).

Last 4 rows: Knit (garter stitch border)

Bind off.

**FINISHING:**
Fold piece in half, wrong sides together.
Seam two sides closed.
Before seaming third side, mark 4 evenly spaced buttonhole positions.
Create buttonholes by separating and reinforcing stitches.
Sew buttons on opposite edge to align.

Insert pillow form and button closed.

**NOTES:**
Ribbed texture adds visual interest and stretch to fit pillow snugly.
Border prevents edges from curling.
